A one-page fact-check · reading time 2 min

The Same-
Page Problem

A DHS one-pager claims 250,000+ non-citizens are registered to vote in four states. The rebuttal is printed three inches below the claim — in the agency's own table.

Exhibit A · The headlinetop of page

"Over 250,000 non-citizens are illegally registered to vote in just the four states for which public data files have been reviewed."

California · Pennsylvania · New Jersey · Nevada — states that don't use DHS's SAVE verification system. Source of the figure: a DHS "review of public voter files." The method is never described.

DHS, "Preventing Alien Voting," July 2026

Exhibit B · The verified numberssame page, lower half

What DHS's own checks find

The bottom half reports results from states that verify registrations through SAVE — DHS's immigration-status tool. Twenty-five states, 68 million records checked:

Exhibit C · The mathbars drawn to scale

Two rates, one page

Headline claim — implied rate ≈0.6%
Method undisclosed

250,000 ÷ ~40M registered voters in CA·PA·NJ·NV

SAVE verification — measured rate 0.04%

28,000 ÷ 68M records actually checked

A ≈15× gap — between a claim and its own evidence

28,000 ÷ 68,000,000  = 0.04%  ← verified
250,000 ÷ ~40,000,000 = ~0.6%  ← claimed
0.6 ÷ 0.04 ≈ 15

Either four states are uniquely fifteen times worse than every state ever verified — including Texas — or the two halves of the page aren't measuring the same thing.

Exhibit D · The precedentwhy the method matters

We've seen this movie

Public voter files contain no citizenship field — a number like 250,000 requires matching names and birthdates against immigration records. Texas tried that in 2019: it flagged ~95,000 "possible non-citizen" voters, counties found within days that tens of thousands were naturalized citizens, and the state rescinded the list and settled in court. That kind of matching mostly finds citizens.

Fair-play notes: Verifying rolls through SAVE is legitimate; cleaning voter lists is good practice. Even the 28,000 are flags pending review, not adjudicated cases. State registration totals are approximate. Every figure on the page measures registration — none measures a vote cast. The 250,000 can't be independently checked, because the method behind it was never published.

Source: DHS, "Preventing Alien Voting," released July 2026 via the White House Government Transparency Task Force. Figures as printed on the document.
